    FIELD NOTES

    How to interpret your capture

    What a four-way handshake tells you

    M1–M4 are pairwise EAPOL-Key messages exchanged by an AP and client. This report groups supported messages by AP/client, descriptor, timing, replay counters, and AP nonce. Four matching messages show that the exchange was observed; they do not validate its cryptography or reveal the Wi-Fi password.

    Partial captures are normal when recording starts mid-connection, packets are missed, or the adapter hops channels. A repeated M1 is still only one observed step.

    No handshakes: is something broken?

    Not necessarily. Connected clients can send traffic without repeating a handshake during your capture. Observe a normal connection on your own network while staying on that AP’s channel. This page analyzes saved traffic and does not initiate connections or transmit packets.

    Unsupported framing can also hide evidence from this decoder. “None observed” does not mean an open or insecure network.

    Decoder coverage and limitations

    The scanner examines every saved packet, independently of the 2,000-row packet preview. It supports unencrypted, unfragmented infrastructure 802.11 and radiotap EAPOL, including QoS headers, standard WPA/RSN descriptors, and 16-byte MIC layouts with descriptor versions 1–3.

    SAE authentication, FT roaming, variable-MIC/AKM-defined layouts, A-MSDU, four-address traffic, Ethernet, and fragmented/encrypted payloads are not assembled into handshakes. Group-key exchanges are listed separately. Flagged errors are excluded from correlation. Use Wireshark for broader decoding.

    Signal strength, retries, and errors

    dBm is logarithmic: −45 dBm is stronger than −75 dBm. Signal reflects reception at your adapter and varies with placement and hardware. A peak is the strongest saved observation, not a constant connection quality.

    Retries are retransmitted frames. Their share is not a packet-loss measurement; capture may miss the original or its ACK. Capture errors indicate suspect observations and do not by themselves identify interference or an attack.

    Networks, devices, and security labels

    SSID is the advertised network name. BSSID identifies an AP radio/interface. Several radios can advertise the same name, and one physical device can use several or randomized MAC addresses.

    Advertised security describes what was observed in network announcements; it does not certify that a network is safe. Device summaries can lag a running capture. Zero recorded clients means no clients were recorded in that summary.

    Frames, channels, and protected content

    Beacons announce networks. Probe requests/responses support discovery. Data frames carry traffic; acknowledgments and other control frames coordinate delivery. Protected frames have encrypted content that this report does not decrypt.

    Channel packet counts depend on capture dwell time, signal, and traffic. They are not channel utilization or a fair congestion comparison when hopping. Captured bytes and PHY rate are not application throughput.

    Read packet evidence in Wireshark

    Download Authentication packets for the detected EAPOL subset or Full packet capture for all traffic. Enter eapol in Wireshark’s display filter; use wlan.fc.retry == 1 for retries. Handshake details provide an AP/client filter.

    “Row” refers to the original Kismet database row, not Wireshark’s frame number. The subset preserves packet bytes and timestamps. Check replay counters, directions, and message sequence alongside the report.

    Imported HC22000 records

    PMKID exports and EAPOL message-pair exports are separate from packet-backed M1–M4 observations. The imported pair label comes from the file; its cryptography and capture completeness are not verified. These files do not supply packet timestamps or channel information.

    Malformed lines are skipped with filename and line-number explanations. Exact normalized records are deduplicated; the first source is retained. Raw PMKIDs, MICs, nonces, and EAPOL blobs are not included in the metadata table or its CSV. Original files remain unchanged. Imports made with the file picker are temporary; CLI imports are embedded into the generated report.

    Search, exports, and snapshot limits

    Search and “Filtered rows” operate on the rows loaded into this page. “All rows · CSV” downloads the complete exported table. The page holds the newest 2,000 packet rows, 5,000 EAPOL rows, and 1,000 handshake attempts; summaries and CSV exports cover the entire capture.

    The authentication PCAPNG contains all EAPOL frames detected by this decoder, including flagged and unclassified observations. An empty subset contains no packet records. Refresh the snapshot with alfa-report --open.
